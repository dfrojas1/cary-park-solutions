The 27th song, and the one real fork in the encore. Every European show this summer closed with a three-song encore — “The Teacher,” then “Exhausted,” then “Everlong.” Toronto cut it to two and dropped “Exhausted.” So its odds here are really the odds of a three-song encore: 45% that DC gets one, and if it does, “Exhausted” is the incumbent that walks back in. It also has the best hometown argument of anything on the bubble — it closes the 1995 debut and it was the first Foo Fighters song ever released to the public, on Eddie Vedder’s Self-Pollution Radio in January 1995, months before “This Is a Call.” Call it 38%, and the single most likely song to push this show past 2:45.

Where the 165 minutes go

Two hours forty-five is 165 minutes, and roughly a fifth of that is not music. Grohl talks — more in DC than anywhere — and the walk out to the B-stage, the covers setup and the encore break each cost real time. That leaves about 131 minutes of playing, which at this band’s stadium pacing is 26 songs.

Hometown wildcards

These are the DC-only adjustments — the reason this projection is not just the Toronto setlist copied over. Grohl was born in Warren, Ohio but raised in Springfield, Virginia; he played his first shows at DC clubs and his first real band, Scream, was a DC hardcore band. On a tour built around covers, that history is loaded and ready.

How the numbers were made

55%

The current tour. Four sampled 2026 shows: the Bridgeport tour opener (Apr 28), BottleRock (May 23), the European leg (June–July) and Toronto (Aug 4). Toronto carries the most weight — it is the only stadium show of this leg and it is 13 days out.

30%

The last five years. Staple rates across the 2021 return shows, the 2023–24 Everything or Nothing at All tour and the 2024 stadium run. This is what pins “Everlong,” “Monkey Wrench,” “My Hero” and “Learn to Fly” near the ceiling — all four sit above 1,000 career plays.

15%

The DC adjustment. Boosts for “The Teacher,” “This Is a Call,” “Marigold” and the cover slot; a small penalty on new-album deep cuts, which get squeezed when a hometown crowd wants the hits.

The honest caveat: only one show of this North American leg has happened. Toronto is a sample of one, and a band that names a tour Take Cover is telling you it intends to change the set. Treat anything under 60% as genuinely open.
