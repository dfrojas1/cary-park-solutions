What 2026 has actually been

The Take Cover tour has run in three distinct chunks, and the set has been rebuilt between each one. That matters: the pool is wide, but the recent shape is narrow.

Documented setlists this year

Aug 4 — TorontoNorth AmericaHits tightened up, five-song band-intro medley added, encore cut to two.26

What “Take Cover” actually means. The tour name is not about rotating city-specific covers — it is a fixed band-introduction medley in which each member plays a song from their own other band. In Toronto that was Chris Shiflett on “Invincible” (No Use for a Name), Nate Mendel on “Seven” (Sunny Day Real Estate), Rami Jaffee on “One Headlight” (The Wallflowers), Pat Smear on “Manimal” (The Germs) and Ilan Rubin on “Tap Dancing in a Minefield” (The New Regime), with Grohl moving to the drum kit.

That is personnel-driven, not city-driven, so it will look much the same in DC. Grohl’s own contribution to the segment is playing drums — which is the Nirvana and Scream nod, without playing either band’s songs.

How the numbers were made

60%

Toronto, Aug 4. The only North American stadium show of this leg, thirteen days before DC, same production and same support bill. When Toronto disagrees with the earlier legs, Toronto wins.

25%

The rest of 2026. Bridgeport (Apr 28) and the European leg (Jun–Jul). Useful for what the band is willing to play, less useful for what they are playing right now — both legs had a different set architecture.

15%

The DC adjustment. Applied only to songs already in the 2026 pool: bumps for “The Teacher,” “This Is a Call,” “Marigold” and “Exhausted,” and a longer expected talk budget.

Three documented setlists is a thin base, and the band has rebuilt the set between every leg this year. Anything below 60% here is a genuine coin flip, and the medley percentages assume the lineup segment survives intact — it is one show old in its five-song form.

Projection assembled Aug 6, 2026, from the three fully documented 2026 setlists. Excludes snippets and jams (“Ace of Spades,” “Da’ Dip,” the “Do It” jam) that are attached to other songs rather than played in full.
